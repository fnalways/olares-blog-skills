# Shared writing rules

These come from line-by-line review feedback on real drafts and apply to every type. Use them alongside the preferences in `olares-blog` `references/writing.md` (no semicolons, no middle dots, no "exciting" or "powerful").

## AI-sounding patterns to avoid

Each of these was flagged in review as "too AI":

- Mirrored reversals: *I hadn't lost the episode. I'd lost everything around it.*
- A colon triplet followed by a summary: *An episode, a book, and a paper: three formats from three places, all about the same question.*
- Announcing the next section: *Here's how they end up in one place.* / *Here's the thing.*
- Aphoristic bridges: *Not every question resolves a page later.* / *Over time, a library like this becomes a close record of…*
- Closing on "so X, not Y": *so the library holds what you chose, not everything that was published.*
- Stacked defensive negatives: *It isn't a bookmark manager. It isn't a feed reader.* Use at most one contrast, for positioning, and state everything else affirmatively.

Instead, state the fact or the action. Replace abstractions with concrete objects: who, what content, which step. Move between paragraphs by picking up the question the previous one left open, not with a slogan.

## Titles and version information

- The H1, SEO title, and cover carry value or positioning, for example *Wise: A Personal Library on Your Own Cloud*.
- Mention the version and any "is back" or "returns" once, in the body: *As of Olares 1.12.8, Wise is available in Olares Market.* Returning users learn about it from the release post.
- A cover subtitle names the reader's situation, not the product category. Weak: *Your library, on your own cloud*. Better: *For everything you meant to come back to*.

## Ecosystem framing

- Present an app as part of the Olares ecosystem, not as a standalone product. Include a section on how it fits Olares, such as LarePass on mobile, running beside your models and agents, or installing from Market. Include only integrations that are confirmed.
- Keep "What's next" restrained. At most: *We'll keep refining the experience and exploring better ways to…*
- Without a real, approved quote, do not include one. Move the stance into a "we" sentence in the body: *Most tools stop at the saving. We built Wise for the part that comes after…*

## Examples and citations

- Make examples concrete, real, and verifiable, but do not narrate screenshots item by item. Naming *Attention Is All You Need* only because it appears in a screenshot reads as forced.
- Avoid dated examples. Citing the Qwen3 technical report in late 2026 felt stale.
- Before citing an external article, check its title, author, and date, and make sure it fits the article's timeline. A June post cannot be described as published days after a November news story.
- First-person experience must match the credited author's real experience. If it does not, use third person or "you".

## Depth

- Favor what and why over a paraphrase of the docs' how. Reduce procedures to one sentence and a docs link.
- A draft usually feels hollow for three reasons: hypothetical scenarios with no concrete objects, feature lists that never say what is different, and statements of stance with no facts behind them. Fix each with specific material.
